The gap we are claiming

Three literatures exist separately, and no paper joins them.

  1. Interactive interpretability interfaces

    They manipulate model internals, but ask “what does this component do?”, never “how sure is this answer, inside?” — LSTMVis, Seq2Seq-Vis, LM-Debugger, LM Transparency Tool, AttentionViz.

  2. Fragility and robustness visual analytics

    It shows how little perturbation flips a prediction, but almost always in input space (adversarial ε) rather than through a causal internal intervention, and it is built for ML practitioners — Bluff, AdvEx, Li et al.

  3. LLM uncertainty communication

    It studies how confidence should be worded or drawn, but the signal is always the output distribution: token probability or verbalized hedging — Kim et al. (FAccT 2024), To Rely or Not to Rely? (CHI 2025).

So mechanistic fragility has never been treated as an uncertainty display, and never evaluated as a comparative design space with non-expert viewers. That is the hole the R0–R4 comparison fills.

Premise support Marjanović, Augenstein & Lioma, Investigating the Impact of Model Instability on Explanations and Uncertainty (Findings of ACL 2024) — explanations shift under perturbations that leave predictive uncertainty looking confident. Internal instability is a real signal that current confidence displays miss.

R3 Push it yourself

Claim Letting a viewer move an intervention themselves and watch the answer swap is an established interpretability technique, and interactivity buys comprehension that a static picture does not.
IEEE TVCG 2019
VAST 2018

Seq2Seq-Vis — Strobelt, Gehrmann, Behrisch, Perer, Pfister, Rush

The canonical what-if on model internals: users change internal state and see the output change. Direct ancestor of R3.

IEEE TVCG 2018

LSTMVis — Strobelt, Gehrmann, Pfister, Rush

Hypothesis-driven probing of hidden state; the user's selection is the query.

EMNLP 2022
system demos

LM-Debugger — Geva, Caciularu, Dar, Roit, Sadde, Shlain, Tamir, Goldberg

Closest existing tool: inspection and intervention in transformer FFN sublayers, “shifting the prediction in a direction of the user's choice”. Ours differs by making the required push size the quantity on display.

ACL 2024 demos verify

LM Transparency Tool — Tufanov, Hambardzumyan, Ferrando, Voita

Interactive input-to-output information flow with attribution to individual heads and neurons. Expert-facing, no uncertainty framing.

CHI 2025 verify

Steerable Chatbots — activation steering as a personalization control

Exposes a steering coefficient as a user-facing slider and studies agency (user-led vs system-led). The strongest precedent that a lay user can be handed a steering strength control.

arXiv 2020
VIS short verify

HypperSteer — Wang & Ma

Interactive perturbation for hypothetical testing on sequence models.

CHI 2016

Prospector — Krause, Perer, Ng

Pre-LLM ancestor: drill into one instance, drag feature values, watch the prediction move. R3 is this, moved from input space to the residual stream.

IEEE TVCG 2020
VAST 2019

The What-If Tool — Wexler, Pushkarna, Bolukbasi, Wattenberg, Viégas, Wilson

Interactive probing as the default idiom for model inspection, including edit-a-datapoint-and-re-infer.

CHI 2019

Explaining Decision-Making Algorithms through UI — Cheng, Wang, Zhang, O'Connell, Gray, Harper, Zhu

The empirical backbone: interactive explanations improved objective and self-reported understanding, while static white-box improved only objective understanding — at the cost of time. This is the rationale for expecting R3 to beat R1, and it names our time-on-task confound.

CHI 2021

Evaluating the Interpretability of Generative Models by Interactive Reconstruction — Ross, Chen, Hang, Glassman, Doshi-Velez

Uses interactive manipulation as the measurement instrument of interpretability — a model for our task design, not just our interface.

arXiv 2021

Explainability Requires Interactivity — Kirchler, Graf, Kloft, Lippert

Position argument that static attributions under-determine model behaviour and counterfactual interaction is required.

R2 Flip curve

Claim Plotting model output against intervention magnitude, and marking where the prediction flips, is an existing and readable way to show fragility; per-layer curves localize it.
IEEE VIS 2020
short

Bluff — Das, Park, Wang, Hohman, Firstman, Rogers, Chau

Shows neuron activation over attack strengths — the same x-axis as our flip curve, in input space.

arXiv 2023/24
venue verify

Panda or not Panda? (AdvEx) — You, Tse, Zhao

A perturbation adjuster for magnitude plus robustness analyzers for accuracy and confidence before and after, built for novices. Our nearest neighbour in both audience and strength-sweep encoding.

CS889 course project
not peer-reviewed

FGSM Explainer — Guo, Tse, Vyas, You

Plots the shift in confidence as the perturbation level rises: the input-space twin of the flip curve, and the clearest existing picture of the encoding. A graduate course project, so use it as design precedent in the text and cite AdvEx instead.

ACM TiiS 2023

Visual Analytics of Neuron Vulnerability to Adversarial Attacks on CNNs — Li, Wang, Fujiwara, Ma

Answers which units are vulnerable — the per-layer-margin question our layer × α heatmap inset is for.

Findings of
EMNLP 2024

Activation Scaling for Steering and Interpreting Language Models — Stoehr, Du, Snæbjarnarson, West, Cotterell, Schein

Treats the scaling factor needed to flip a prediction as the quantity of interest: the computational sibling of our margin, and the formal reason to sweep α at all.

Worth one sentence in the prose: perturbation-magnitude curves are already standard practice in explanation evaluation (deletion and AOPC-style curves), so the encoding is not exotic to an ML reader.

R1 Margin score

Claim Collapsing fragility to a single banded score is defensible, and the uncertainty-visualization literature says how to encode it without over-claiming precision — which matters because our margin is an approximation (ρ ≈ 0.7 with output confidence).
IJCAI 2020

DeepView — Schulz, Hinder, Hammer

Distance to the decision boundary plus per-point certainty as the visual unit. Our margin is a causal-space version of “how far from flipping”.

Handbook
chapter 2020

Uncertainty Visualization — Padilla, Kay, Hullman

The frame for “which representation of the same uncertainty?”, which is our whole manipulation.

CHI 2016

When (ish) is My Bus? — Kay, Kola, Hullman, Munson

Lay viewers read quantified uncertainty better in frequency and discrete framings than in abstract intervals.

CHI 2018

Uncertainty Displays Using Quantile Dotplots or CDFs Improve Transit Decision-Making — Fernandes, Walls, Munson, Hullman, Kay

A display that improves estimation is not automatically the one that improves decisions. This is why we measure prediction accuracy and not only comprehension.

IEEE TVCG 2021
VIS 2020

Visual Reasoning Strategies for Effect Size Judgments and Decisions — Kale, Nguyen, Kay, Hullman

Same lesson with strategy-level qualitative analysis; a model for coding our open responses, where many participants switch strategies.

CHI 2018

Value-Suppressing Uncertainty Palettes — Correll, Moritz, Heer

How to keep a fragility score from reading as more certain than it is.

Human Factors
2005

Displaying Uncertainty — Bisantz et al.

Older, still useful: viewers comprehended the familiar bar best yet did not find it most helpful — the R0-against-R1 split between preference and performance.

R4 Counterfactual card

Claim A one-sentence counterfactual (“a small nudge toward Rio changes the answer”) is a validated explanation format for non-experts, and “the smallest change that flips it” is its usual content.
IEEE TVCG 2021

DECE — Cheng, Ming, Qu

Counterfactuals as the user-facing unit, with interactions for customizing them; argues the format is human-friendly and actionable.

CHI 2023

GAM Coach — Wang, Wortman Vaughan, Caruana, Chau

Users slide feature values under their own constraints until the prediction flips. R3 and R4 are the two halves of this interaction, split apart so we can test them separately.

Frontiers in
Comp. Sci. 2023

Let's Go to the Alien Zoo — Kuhl, Artelt, Hammer

A reusable study framework plus evidence that counterfactual explanations beat no explanation on objective performance and subjective usability, and are rated more satisfying and trustworthy than causal explanations.

arXiv 2022

Keep Your Friends Close and Your Counterfactuals Closer — Kuhl, Artelt, Hammer

Closest counterfactuals teach better than merely plausible ones — support for reporting the minimum push rather than a vivid one.

arXiv 2024

Interactive Counterfactual Generation for Univariate Time Series

Recent precedent for interactive counterfactual authoring outside tabular data.

R0 Baseline and the Overview scatter

Claim Output-probability confidence is the right baseline because it is what the field studies and what products ship; plotting the mechanistic signal against it is the contribution.
FAccT 2024

“I'm Not Sure, But…” — Kim, Liao, Vorvoreanu, Ballard, Wortman Vaughan

n = 404: first-person uncertainty expressions lowered agreement with the system and raised accuracy. The canonical result our R0 condition inherits.

CHI 2025

To Rely or Not to Rely? — interventions for appropriate reliance on LLMs

Reliance interventions reduce over-reliance but mostly fail to produce appropriate reliance. This sets the bar our fragility views must clear, and warns us off claiming a trust gain.

arXiv 2026

Not All Uncertainty Is Equal — how uncertainty granularity shapes human verification

Token-, output- and relation-level uncertainty move agreement and self-confidence differently. Our per-layer curve against a single score is the mechanistic analogue of granularity.

arXiv 2026

When Should I Trust This? Factuality Indicators for LLM-based Chatbots

The most recent comparison of indicator formats — numeric, categorical, natural language — which is the design space R0, R1 and R4 sit in.

CHI 2023

Zeno — Cabrera, Fu, Bertucci, Holstein, Talwalkar, Hong, Perer

Behavioural evaluation as interactive slicing; the framing for our Overview quadrants, where “confident but fragile” is a slice.

CHI 2023

Angler — Robertson, Wang, Moritz, Kery, Hohman

Prioritizing by where the model is weak; supports the one-sentence takeaway above the scatter.

IEEE TVCG 2024
VIS 2023

AttentionViz — Yeh, Chen, Wu, Chen, Viégas, Wattenberg

Global-view-plus-detail structure for transformer internals: precedent for our overview-to-item drill-down.

Method papers for the study itself

  • Cheng et al., CHI 2019 — interactive against static, within-subjects comprehension measures, and the time trade-off.
  • Ross et al., CHI 2021 — interaction-based interpretability measurement.
  • Kuhl et al., 2023 (Alien Zoo) — a powered, controlled XAI user-study framework.
  • Fernandes et al., CHI 2018 and Kale et al., TVCG 2021 — separating estimation accuracy from decision quality, plus strategy coding for open responses.
  • Explanation User Interfaces: A Systematic Literature Review (arXiv 2505.20085) — for positioning the design space and checking we are not missing a view family.
